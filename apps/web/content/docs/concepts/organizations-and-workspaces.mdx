---
title: Organizations and Workspaces
description: Understand how Namera groups accounts, members, and connected applications.
---

A **workspace** groups your Namera accounts, session keys, members, client
authorizations, and billing. The API calls this an **organization**. These are
two names for the same boundary, not separate levels of a hierarchy.

## Your Active Workspace

You can belong to multiple workspaces. Your dashboard session selects one active
workspace at a time, and its pages show the resources you are allowed to access
there.

Switching workspaces changes that context. It does not move accounts, transfer
funds, or give a connected application access to the newly selected workspace.
If an expected account is missing, check the active workspace and your membership.

## Members and Roles

Your role determines which management actions you can perform in the workspace.
Namera has built-in **Owner**, **Admin**, and **Member** roles. The Member role
is read-oriented; management actions such as creating accounts or granting
client access require the corresponding permissions.

A person who can manage members cannot assign authority beyond their own.
Account ownership remains separate: having a workspace role does not give you
another person's owner passkey or replace an account's required owner approval.

Membership and permissions are checked on subsequent requests. Losing workspace
access does not transfer an account's assets or change its onchain owner.

## Connected Applications

An API key, CLI connection, or MCP connection receives grants to selected
session keys. It does not inherit the dashboard user's entire workspace role.

For example, a workspace can contain accounts for several projects while one
agent is authorized to use only a session key for a single account. Its accessible
accounts and operations follow that grant and the key's permissions.

Changing the active workspace in your browser does not change a saved CLI or MCP
connection. Review that connection's authorization when changing its access.

## Workspace Owner and Account Owner

The **workspace Owner** is a membership role in Namera. The **account owner** is
the signing authority that approves onchain permission changes. One describes
management access; the other describes control of a smart account.

Keep these boundaries separate when planning team access. Removing a member or
revoking an app connection is not the same operation as removing a session key's
onchain permissions.

Continue with [Accounts](/docs/concepts/accounts) for account ownership or
[Permissions and Policies](/docs/concepts/permissions-and-policies) for delegated
authority.
